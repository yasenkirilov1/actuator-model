# Real voice-coil examples versus the proposed flange envelope

Reviewed 2026-09-30. This bounded manufacturer-source check executed **zero native simulations** and **zero hardware measurements**. It does not qualify a part or provide a purchasing list. The comparison target is approximately 60 N continuous, at least 70 N peak, 25 mm stroke, motor constant Kf/√R near 10 N/√W or better, and a moving assembly that leaves space within B = 0.6 kg for the holder, guides, force sensor and wiring.

**A lighter real moving-coil example exists:** Moticont LVCM-070-051-01 has a 240 g coil assembly, rather than the 755 g moving assembly of the retained Sensata example. Its center-stroke ratings are close to the desired force/efficiency envelope. It is not a proven drop-in replacement: its stationary body is 1.24 kg, its published continuous-force curve falls near the ends, and its winding has materially different resistance and inductance. No lightweight moving-magnet part meeting the full force target was established by this bounded check; a custom moving-magnet design remains possible.

The Moticont drawing was visually inspected because its PDF has no searchable text. It specifies a 69.9 mm body diameter, 50.8 mm housing length and about 76.2 mm overall length at midstroke. Its **continuous-force versus stroke** graph is roughly 52 N at the travel ends and peaks near the listed 64.7 N at the center; these endpoint values are approximate graph readings. This does not establish an exact Kf(x) curve, equal force in both directions or the installed thermal boundary. If the prototype requires 60 N continuous throughout all25.4 mm, this drawing does not prove it. A centrally used travel window might suffice, but must be defined from actual operating displacement and measured force/current/temperature maps.

The Moticont sheet lists maximum continuous power45 W and states its values are at25°C. Required mounting, airflow, maximum winding temperature and hot-force derating were not established in the retrieved drawing. At center, the simple cold calculation for60 N is I =60/24.4 =2.459 A and I²R =38.70 W; at70 N it is2.869 A,18.36 V resistive drop and52.67 W. Thus70 N is peak effort under this power provision. At24 V, only5.64 V remains above the70 N cold resistive drop for inductive slew and back EMF, before amplifier losses. Higher voltage can improve slew when clipping occurs, but cannot remove copper heating.

For comparison, the Sensata drawing specifies force sensitivity ±10%, R ±12.5% and L ±30%. Its continuous-stall rating is explicitly at25°C ambient /155°C coil; peak is a10-second condition at those temperatures. Those tolerances and operating conditions must not be replaced by perfectly identified20 N/A and100 µs response assumptions. Its integrated sensor is also not evidence for the proposed1 µm position channel. The drawing confirms the755 g coil assembly, so it must not be silently put inside B0.6 kg.

One moving-magnet negative reference is [H2W NCM10-14-028-2BX](https://www.h2wtech.com/product/voice-coil-actuators/NCM10-14-028-2BX): 25.4 mm stroke and106 g moving mass, but only12.5 N continuous /37.4 N at10% duty and4.6 N/√W. It demonstrates that a light magnet assembly is possible, while this particular part fails the force and efficiency targets. The result is not a claim that all moving-magnet designs fail. H2W's [architecture description](https://www.h2wtech.com/category/voice-coil-actuators) distinguishes moving-coil designs requiring external guidance from moving-magnet designs with a shaft/bearings and no moving power leads.

For the prototype recommendation, retain a **specification envelope** rather than claiming that a catalog part is already qualified: at least60 N continuous under the installed cooling condition and required position range, at least70 N transient with identified duration/duty, approximately25 mm useful stroke, motor constant near10 N/√W or better, and moving motor mass preferably≤250–300 g within the complete B0.6 kg assembly. Stationary mass must be added to A and the robot payload. Include force-versus-position, hot R/Kf, inductance, cable force, guide friction and real current-loop delay in the native candidate. A measured or identified winding must be used to retune the observer; a manufacturer's peak force does not establish100 µs current response.

The Moticont motor provides a defensible physical reference for that next model check. A successful native run using its nominal Kf/R/L would remain conditional on cooling, complete mechanical mass and measured sensing/drive dynamics.
